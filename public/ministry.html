What the gap costs, and what closes it

Arithmetic, not projection

Every figure here is derived from the district table below — no new assumption is introduced. It is the argument for doing anything at all.

01A mark sheet is imported. Whatever the school already has — a term test, the Grade Four Assessment — read row by row, with anything unreadable reported rather than dropped.
02Scores become a baseline per strand, recency-weighted. Not one mark for Mathematics — a number for Number Concepts, and a different one for Geometry.
03Strands under expectation become goals, each with a target and a criterion. Deterministic; no model decides a baseline or a goal.
04Goals pull modules from the grade materials — including earlier years, so a Grade 6 pupil is taught at the level the evidence puts her at.
05Every checkpoint updates the plan, and rolls up to the school, the district and this page. The gap is measured out of the same records that drive the teaching.

What this page cannot yet tell you. That chain is built and working; whether it moves the national gap faster than 1.5 points a year is not something any demonstration can show. That is the pilot's question — forty schools across four districts, measured against a matched comparison group, with a decision point at month seven.
