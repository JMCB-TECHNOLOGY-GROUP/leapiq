Seeing every child in time to act

The Ministry cannot currently see which child is behind, in which strand, in which region, until the national assessment says so — at Grade Six, for a problem that usually began in Grade One. LeapIQ is the national instrument that closes that gap between what is happening in classrooms and what the Ministry can see and act on.

How it works — with the materials the Ministry already has

1A mark sheet is imported — any test the school already gives, read row by row; anything unreadable is reported, never silently dropped.
2Scores become a baseline for every curriculum strand — not one mark for Mathematics, a number for Number Concepts and a different one for Geometry.
3Strands below expectation become an individual plan — measurable goals that pull modules from pre-loaded national class materials for every grade, reaching back to earlier years so each child is taught at the level the evidence shows, not the level they are enrolled in.
4Every piece of work updates the plan — and rolls up, from the child to the class to the school to the district to the Ministry, nightly.

Nobody types a report. The work is the report.

Built for Guyana's real constraints

  • Offline-first: a school with no connection for a full term loses nothing — the plan engine runs on the device.
  • Child-safe by construction: no email identities for children; a data-protection impact assessment signed before any pupil record moves.
  • NCERD owns the curriculum: the platform loads the Ministry's strands and objectives as versioned content, and every AI-generated practice item is reviewed by a Guyanese teacher before it reaches a child.
  • Teachers are the rollout: cascade training led by serving Guyanese master trainers; the teacher receives a ranked list of who to sit with — never another system to feed.

The programme

  • US$7.5M over five years, total cost of ownership — 0.26% of the 2026 education budget in its peak year.
  • National primary coverage inside 12 months: all 436 primary schools, 50,000 pupils in Grades 4–6.
  • All 1,155 public schools by month 30, Nursery 1 to Form 5, with handover to Ministry operation beginning at month 22.
  • Every phase ends with a result the Ministry can verify — and none commits the country to the next.

What we propose today

A pilot, not a programme: forty schools across four districts — two coastal, two hinterland — for five months, measured against a matched comparison group, with a decision point at month seven that the Ministry controls. If it does not show a lift, it stops, and it costs the pilot rather than the programme.

Needed in month one  ·  the NCERD curriculum guides with official objective numbering  ·  a school census extract  ·  a data-sharing agreement and DPIA sign-off. None of the three costs money.
